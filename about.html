About Brine

Hi, I'm Aaron. I've been making things my whole life.

First it was Lego. Then it was YouTube videos. Now it's content for local businesses that people actually watch to the end.

My story

It started with Lego

My first creative outlet.

Lego was my first passion, and it still is. I've been building since I was a kid, and my collection has only grown. Lego teaches you to picture something before it exists, then put it together one piece at a time until it looks right.

That's how I still work. A good video is built the same way: a plan, the right pieces, and a lot of small decisions that add up to something people want to look at.

Then came Pickle Pouch

Learning video the hard way, on YouTube.

Before Brine, I made videos as a YouTuber under the name Pickle Pouch. On YouTube, nobody owes you their attention. If a video is slow for even a second, people swipe away, and the numbers show you exactly where they left.

I spent a lot of time studying those numbers. I filmed, edited, posted, checked where viewers dropped off, and tried again. Doing that over and over is what turned me into an editor.

What it taught me

How people actually watch short videos.

YouTube pushed me to learn the psychology behind short-form content: how the brain decides in the first second whether to keep watching, how much information people can take in at once, and what makes them stay until the end.

I use that in every video I make for clients. The goal is simple: keep viewers watching, keep retention high, and get the most out of every video you pay for.

How it helps you

What YouTube taught me, built into your videos.

The first second

Every video opens with a hook that gives people a reason to stop scrolling before they've decided to move on.

Pacing that holds

Tight cuts, on-screen text and movement every few seconds, so nothing gives a viewer a reason to leave.

One idea at a time

The brain can only take in so much at once. I keep each video focused on one clear message, so people remember your business, not just the video.

Why "Brine"?

It goes back to Pickle Pouch. Brine is what turns a plain cucumber into a pickle. That's what good content should do for your business: give it flavor people remember.
